## Team Collaboration

Anoushka contributed to the initial structure of the project, creating the first initial scatterplot visualization for female tertiary education rates as well as implementing the chloropleth graph that serves as an introduction to our intended problem. Additionally, Anoushka contributed to the Storylines visualization, which maps how declining fertility rates in the countries that have seen the most drop since 1990 compare with other socioeconomic factors. Anoushka also styled the visualization to model how visualizations are presented on popular websites like the New York Times. 

Omeed contributed to the lifestyle visualization, which presents an animated playback of how fertility has responded to digital modernity in the form of a scatterplot. He also contributed to many of the D3.js animations involved throughout the visualization, most notably how hovering above an item results in a tick displayed in the legend. He also worked on the initial project proposal - figuring out the specific goal the project would target, collecting some initial datasets from the World Bank, and providing an initial plan to create an interactive website visualization. In addition to this, he also worked on creating a demo video (in case of any presentation errors) and formulating key points from each visualization for better group understanding and explainability in a class setting. 

William contributed to an initial implementation of a chloropleth graph, which made headway for the chloropleth globe displayed on the introduction visualization, and also did much of the data preprocessing involved in combining CSVs from multiple data sources into one singular CSV. William also contributed to adding more functionality to the initial scatterplot visualization, also adding a feature to view how fertility rate compares with the GDP of a nation. 

All group members contributed to the implementation of the public spending visualization, separately taking on the implementation of the bar charts and corresponding dots, which mark fertility rates, and legend items. 

When creating this visualization, we wanted to make it such that adding additional functionality was a simple process. That is how we arrived at the dashboard style currently implemented, where each separate visualization was implemented simply as a matter of appending it to the rest. We then brainstormed different socioeconomic theories we wanted to tackle regarding birthrate decline, with the ultimate goal of displaying how no one factor dominates this issue and that resolving it is more complex than one would think.
