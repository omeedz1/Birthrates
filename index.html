The Global Fertility Decline And Its Contributing Factors

By Anoushka Arora, Omeed Zolali, and William Amorosi

Fertility Rates Have Fallen Across Much Of The World Since 1990

Around the world, birth rates are falling—and the reasons are more complex than a single cause. As education levels rise, economies shift, cities grow, and the costs of raising children increase, many people are choosing to have fewer children or to delay parenthood. This trend is shaped by a wide range of social, economic, cultural, and personal factors that influence how people think about family life. This visualization explores the many forces behind declining birth rates and how they vary across countries, regions, and generations.

Fertility Decline Appears Broad-Based Across Education And GDP Levels

Although countries with higher education levels or GDP per capita tend to have lower fertility rates, the relationship is not as straightforward as it may seem. Use the x-axis dropdown to compare fertility against each variable. In both views, the broad decline over time suggests that neither education nor GDP alone explains the global fertility shift.

Family Policy Spending And Fertility

Public family spending varies widely across countries, both in total scale and in the mix of cash payments, services, and tax breaks. This view shows each country's family benefits as a share of GDP, with fertility overlaid as a dot so the spending mix and fertility level can be compared country by country.
